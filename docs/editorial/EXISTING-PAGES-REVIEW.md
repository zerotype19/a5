## Drywall repair for holes, cracks, and damaged ceilings

Page: `drywall` · SERVICE

### Can damaged drywall be repaired without replacing the whole wall?

A localized hole or damaged joint may be repairable without replacing the entire wall. The provider needs to assess the size, condition and cause of the damage, then discuss finishing and paint. If water is involved, address the source before repairing the surface.

Get help with wall holes, damaged seams and ceiling repairs, with the finishing work considered from the start. Share the location and size of the damage, whether it has been repaired before and whether water is involved. A5 reviews the details and checks for a provider to assess the repair and the next steps.

### What the provider will assess

A small hole may need little more than patching and finishing.

A larger hole can be cut back to solid drywall and filled with a new piece.

A recurring crack may need the joint re-taped rather than simply filled again.

Water-damaged drywall is different: the water problem gets fixed first.

### Holes

The size and condition of the drywall determine the repair.

Tiny nail and screw holes are simple.

A doorknob-sized hole usually needs a proper patch.

A larger opening may need a new piece of drywall secured to backing or framing.

You generally don't need to replace an entire wall because of one damaged area.

### Cracks

Drywall cracks are common, especially around doors, windows, ceilings, and joints between sheets.

Many are cosmetic.

A crack that keeps coming back often needs more than another layer of filler. The old joint may need to be opened, secured, re-taped, and refinished.

If the crack is widening or appears alongside sticking doors, sloping floors, or other movement, mention that too.

### Water-damaged ceilings and walls

The order matters:

1. Stop the leak.

2. Let the area dry or remove wet material.

3. Repair the drywall.

4. Prime and paint.

A dry stain on solid drywall may not require replacing the board.

Soft, sagging, crumbling, or moldy drywall usually does.

If the source is plumbing, A5 can coordinate that step before the drywall repair. If it's coming from the roof, you'll need a roofer first.

### Will the repair disappear?

A good drywall repair should be flat and difficult to see once it's finished. But drywall compound isn't the final surface. The area still needs primer and paint, and matching an older paint color or wall texture can be as important as the patch itself.

### Drywall or plaster?

If you're not sure what your walls are made of, don't worry about diagnosing it. Photograph the damaged area — especially an exposed edge if there is one — and mention that you're unsure. Plaster and drywall are repaired differently, so the professional can confirm what you have before work begins.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The full wall or ceiling around the damage.
- **Include the detail** The hole or crack with an object for scale, without reaching into it.
- **Add useful context** A previous patch, surrounding texture and what is above any water damage.

## Cracked or crumbling brick steps

Page: `brick-step-repair` · PROBLEM

### Can my brick steps be repaired, or do they need rebuilding?

A provider needs to assess the condition of the steps and the support beneath them before recommending repair or rebuilding. Mention missing mortar, loose treads, sinking, tilting or separation from the house. Photos help start the assessment but cannot establish structural stability.

Brick steps rarely fail all at once. Usually the first signs are small: mortar starts disappearing, the face of a brick flakes off, a tread loosens, or a gap opens where the steps meet the house.

### Signs this may be a repair

A repair may be enough when:

Mortar is cracked or missing but the steps remain level.

A few bricks are cracked or deteriorated.

A tread or cap has loosened.

The steps still feel solid underfoot.

There isn't meaningful separation from the house.

Typical work can include repointing mortar joints, replacing damaged bricks and resetting loose treads or caps.

### Signs the steps may need to be rebuilt

Look more closely if:

The whole set of steps leans or tilts.

One side has noticeably sunk.

The top landing has pulled away from the house.

Cracks run through several courses of brick.

Repairs have repeatedly failed in the same area.

Those signs can point to settlement or a failing base. In that case, repeatedly patching mortar is unlikely to last.

### Water matters

Brick and mortar are exposed to rain, snow and repeated freezing and thawing. Water from an overflowing gutter or a downspout emptying next to the steps can make deterioration worse. If the base is washing out or staying wet, correcting the water problem may be part of making the masonry repair last.

### If a step is loose

Treat a loose or rocking tread as a trip hazard. Use another entrance if practical until it can be repaired.

Avoid filling failing mortar joints with caulk or general patching compound. It can make proper masonry repair harder later.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The full staircase from the front.
- **Include the detail** Damaged brick or mortar and a side view showing alignment.
- **Add useful context** The junction with the house and nearby gutters or downspouts.

### What affects the job

The biggest cost difference is repair versus partial or complete rebuild. Size matters too: number and width of steps, side walls, landings and railings all add work. Brick matching can matter on older homes, and rebuilding the base adds excavation and material. Sometimes only one section needs rebuilding while the rest can be repaired.

## Mortar falling out between bricks

Page: `loose-mortar` · PROBLEM

### Does crumbling mortar mean the brickwork needs rebuilding?

Crumbling mortar does not by itself establish that the whole area needs rebuilding. The provider should check the condition of the bricks and whether the masonry has moved before recommending repointing. Mention leaning, loose bricks or a repair that has failed again.

### Signs it may just need repointing

Look for:

Sandy or crumbling mortar.

Joints that have worn back from the face of the brick.

Small cracks following the mortar joints.

Gaps where mortar has fallen out.

Otherwise solid, stable brick.

### Signs there may be more going on

Pay more attention if:

Bricks are loose.

The wall is leaning or bulging.

Cracks run through the bricks themselves.

A retaining wall is moving.

The same area keeps failing after repairs.

Those can point to movement or another underlying problem rather than ordinary mortar wear.

### Why mortar fails

Mortar is designed to be the more sacrificial part of traditional brickwork. Over time it takes the wear from weather and moisture. Water can accelerate that deterioration, particularly around gutters, downspouts, ground-level masonry, steps, and other areas that stay wet. Previous repairs can matter too. Mortar that's too hard for older brick can shift damage into the brick itself.

### What repointing involves

Failed mortar is removed from the joints and replaced with new mortar that's appropriate for the masonry. If individual bricks are cracked or damaged, those can often be replaced at the same time. If the wall has moved substantially, however, simply replacing the mortar won't correct the underlying issue.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The full wall, steps or walkway.
- **Include the detail** The worst mortar joints and any cracking.
- **Add useful context** Any visible bowing, movement or nearby downspouts.

## Tile repair without redoing the whole room

Page: `tile` · SERVICE

### Can individual tiles or damaged grout be repaired?

A localized repair may be possible when the surrounding tile and backing are sound. Matching replacement tile, movement and moisture can change the scope. Share photos and mention any spare tiles so the provider can discuss repair options before proposing a larger project.

Start with the tile or grout that needs attention, without assuming the whole room needs replacing. Send photos of the damaged area and any spare tiles, and mention movement or moisture. A5 reviews your request and checks for a provider who can assess the condition and discuss a suitable repair scope.

### What the provider will assess

A cracked tile can frequently be replaced individually.

Loose tile can sometimes be reset.

Failing grout can often be removed and replaced.

The two questions that matter most are:

Can the tile be matched?

Is the surface underneath still solid and dry?

### If you have spare tile, find it

Check the basement, garage, attic, or utility room. Builders and previous owners often leave extra tile behind, and a few matching pieces can turn a difficult repair into a straightforward one. If there's a box, photograph the label. Without spares, common tile may still be available. Older or discontinued tile can be much harder to match exactly.

### Cracked or loose tile

One or two damaged tiles don't automatically mean the whole floor or wall needs to come out. If the surrounding tile is solid and the surface underneath is sound, the damaged pieces can often be removed and replaced. If many tiles sound hollow, move when pressed, or crack along the same line, the issue may be underneath the tile rather than with the individual pieces.

### Crumbling grout

Grout can wear out while the tile itself remains perfectly usable. If the tiles are well bonded, failed grout can often be removed and replaced. If the grout keeps cracking in the same place, however, movement underneath may be causing it.

### Showers need a closer look

Tile and grout are the visible finish of a shower, not the entire waterproofing system. Cracked grout doesn't automatically mean there's water damage behind the wall. But loose tile, a soft wall, persistent moisture, or staining on the other side deserves a closer look. If a plumbing leak is involved, that gets addressed separately.

### Small repair or larger project?

A few tiles with good backing and available replacements usually point to a localized repair. A large area of loose tile, a flexing floor, wet backing, or no acceptable match may make rebuilding more of the surface the better choice. The goal isn't to replace more than necessary. It's to avoid repairing the visible tile while leaving the real problem underneath.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The whole floor, backsplash or shower wall.
- **Include the detail** Damaged grout or tile and any visible signs of movement.
- **Add useful context** Spare tile, its box label and any relevant staining on the opposite wall.

## Sunken or rocking pavers

Page: `sunken-pavers` · PROBLEM

### Can existing pavers be reused in a repair?

Existing pavers may be reusable if they are in suitable condition. The repair scope depends on the affected area, the base and any water or movement issues. Show the whole walkway or patio as well as the low spot so the provider can assess more than the visible surface.

### What you may notice

A low spot that holds water.

One or more pavers rocking underfoot.

Raised edges that create a trip point.

Joints opening near the edge.

Sections of the patio or walkway that no longer look level.

### Why it happens

The base settled. The gravel or bedding layer underneath can compact over time.

Water washed material away. Downspouts and runoff can undermine a section.

The edge moved. Without good edge restraint, pavers can spread outward.

Roots pushed from below.

Freeze-thaw movement changed the base.

### Small repair or bigger rebuild?

A small, clearly defined low area can often be lifted and reset. If the entire patio has waves, widespread sinking, or persistent drainage problems, more of the base may need to be rebuilt. Even then, good existing pavers can often be reused.

- **Show the whole area** The full patio or walkway.
- **Include the detail** The uneven section and a side view of the level change.
- **Add useful context** Water after rain, nearby trees, slopes or downspouts.

### If there is an active leak

Use a known water shutoff only if you can reach and operate it safely. Keep away from water near electrical equipment. If the water cannot be controlled, contact an emergency plumber rather than waiting for an A5 response. Take photos only once it is safe to do so.

### Repair or replace?

Many plumbing problems are repairs, not replacements. A dripping faucet may need a cartridge, washer, or another internal part. A plumbing repair may stop the leak without finishing the job.

Damaged drywall.

A stained ceiling.

Peeling paint.

Wet cabinets.

Damaged tile or flooring.

The order matters:

Fix the leak, then let the area dry, then repair the damaged surface, then paint or finish last.

A5 can help keep those related steps connected instead of treating them as unrelated projects.

No longer keeping up with normal use.

## Hole in a wall

Page: `hole-in-drywall` · PROBLEM

### Can a hole be patched without replacing the whole wall?

Many localized holes can be repaired without replacing the entire wall. The size of the opening, condition of the surrounding board and anything exposed behind it affect the repair. Include the wall texture and paint in the conversation so the estimate covers the finished result.

Small holes can be filled. A doorknob-sized hole can be patched. Larger damaged areas can be cut back to solid drywall and replaced with a new piece.

You generally do not need to replace an entire wall because of a hole.

The part that separates a good repair from an obvious patch is the finishing: getting the surface flat, matching any texture, and painting it so the repair disappears.

### The size changes the repair

Nail and screw holes. Usually filled, sanded, primed, and touched up.

Small impact or doorknob holes. Typically need backing or a patch plus several thin coats of joint compound.

Larger holes. The damaged drywall is cut into a clean shape, backing or framing is added where needed, and a new piece of drywall is installed, taped, and finished.

Soft or water-damaged drywall. That's different. The damaged material may need to come out, but the water source needs to be fixed first.

### Will the patch show?

The drywall repair itself should be flat and hard to find once it's finished. Paint is often the bigger challenge. Even if you have the original paint color, older paint can fade or change slightly over time. On a very visible wall, painting from corner to corner can give a cleaner result than touching up only the patch.

### What if there are wires or pipes behind it?

Don't poke or cut farther into the wall. If the hole exposes plumbing, electrical wiring, or an opening made during another repair, mention that in the request. It can change how the repair should be approached.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The whole wall.
- **Include the detail** The hole with an object for scale, without cutting or probing it.
- **Add useful context** Surrounding texture, paint and any other holes in the request.

## Water stain or soft spot on the ceiling

Page: `water-damaged-ceiling` · PROBLEM

### What should be fixed first when a ceiling has water damage?

The water source needs attention before the ceiling is refinished. Keep clear of a wet, bulging or sagging area and describe whether the leak is active or already repaired. Once the source is addressed, a professional can assess drying, damaged material and the finishing work required.

The stain is where you see the problem. It isn't necessarily where the problem started.

### If it is wet right now

Keep people away from sagging or bulging areas and from water near electrical equipment. Do not puncture the ceiling or investigate behind a light fixture. An uncontrolled leak needs emergency help rather than a scheduled A5 request.

### Where the water may be coming from

Below a bathroom, common sources include a toilet seal, shower or tub, drain, supply line or another plumbing connection. On a top-floor ceiling, the source could be the roof, flashing, a gutter issue or condensation in the attic. Sometimes the leak has already been repaired and all that's left is the stain. The ceiling still needs to be completely dry before it's sealed and painted.

### Does the drywall need to come out?

Not always. If the ceiling is dry, firm and flat, the repair may be mostly cosmetic: seal the stain with the right primer and repaint. If the drywall is soft, swollen, sagging or crumbling, the damaged section is usually cut out. The cavity can then dry before new drywall is installed and finished. Visible or extensive mold may require remediation beyond ordinary drywall repair.

### Why this can involve more than one trade

Water-damaged ceilings are a good example of why A5 starts with the problem instead of asking homeowners to choose the contractor. A plumbing leak may need a plumber first. The opening then needs drywall repair. Finally, the ceiling may need to be painted so the repair disappears. A5 can coordinate those steps in the right order. If the source is the roof, you'll need a roofer first; roofing is not currently an A5 service.

### Photos that help

- **Show the whole area** The ceiling and its position within the room.
- **Include the detail** Visible staining or damage from a safe location; stay out from under sagging areas.
- **Add useful context** What is above the affected area and any nearby fixture.

### What affects the job

The biggest variable is the source of the water. After that, scope depends on whether the ceiling only needs stain treatment and paint or whether drywall has to be removed, replaced and finished. Ceiling height, texture and whether the entire ceiling needs repainting can also change the amount of work.

## Wall or ceiling crack that keeps coming back

Page: `drywall-crack` · PROBLEM

### Why does a repaired drywall crack keep returning?

A returning crack needs an assessment of the joint and the cause of movement, rather than another surface patch alone. Tell the provider when it appeared, whether it is changing and what previous repairs were made. Widening cracks or other new movement should be assessed before cosmetic work.

Many drywall cracks are cosmetic — especially along seams, above doors and windows, or where walls meet ceilings. A lasting repair often means opening the crack, repairing or replacing the tape, and refinishing the joint rather than just filling the visible line.

The important question is whether you're looking at a drywall problem or signs of larger movement.

### Usually a drywall repair

Common examples include:

A thin crack over the corner of a door or window.

A straight crack along a drywall seam.

A crack where the wall meets the ceiling.

Nail or screw pops.

A crack that reappeared through an old patch.

These can often be repaired by securing the drywall where needed, re-taping the joint, applying new compound, sanding, priming, and painting.

### When to look closer

Tell us if the crack: Is getting noticeably wider. Appeared suddenly. Runs through several different surfaces. Is accompanied by doors that recently started sticking. Appears with sloping floors or foundation cracking. That doesn't automatically mean there's a structural problem. It does mean fixing the drywall may not be the first question to answer.

### Why drywall cracks

Homes move slightly over time. Wood framing expands and contracts. Drywall joints take some of that movement. Fasteners can shift. Older tape can lose its bond. Previous repairs may have covered the crack without correcting the joint underneath. That's why the location and pattern of the crack matter more than simply whether a crack exists.

### Will the repair show?

A properly finished repair should blend into the surrounding wall once it's primed and painted. Texture and paint matching can make the finishing work more involved, particularly on ceilings or walls that haven't been painted in years.

### Photos that help

A few clear photos can help start the conversation. Only photograph areas you can reach safely.

- **Show the whole area** The full crack from end to end.
- **Include the detail** Its width and any nearby nail pops or previous patch.
- **Add useful context** The adjoining door, window or ceiling line; mention a door that has also started sticking.

## Toilet that keeps running

Page: `running-toilet` · PROBLEM

### Does a running toilet need repair or replacement?

A running toilet may have a repairable problem with a component inside the tank. Describe whether it runs continuously, refills between uses or also leaks outside the fixture. A plumber can assess the parts and overall condition before recommending repair or replacement.

You flush, the tank fills — and then the water never quite stops. Or the toilet suddenly refills for a few seconds even though nobody used it.

### Describe when the water runs

Tell the plumber whether the toilet runs continuously after flushing or briefly refills when nobody has used it. Mention any water around the base as a separate observation. EPA identifies worn toilet flappers as one possible source of leaks, but the plumber needs to determine the cause for your fixture.

### What usually gets repaired

Common repairs include:

Replacing the flapper.

Replacing or adjusting the fill valve.

Adjusting the float.

### Can damaged masonry be repaired, or does it need rebuilding?

Repair options depend on both the visible damage and the stability of the masonry beneath it. Describe loose mortar, damaged bricks, uneven pavers or any change in alignment. A provider can assess whether a localized repair is appropriate or whether the base needs attention.

### What the provider will assess

The key is figuring out what actually failed.

If the mortar is deteriorating but the brickwork is still solid, repointing may be enough.

If a few bricks or pavers are damaged, those pieces can often be replaced or reset.

If the entire section has tilted, sunk, or pulled away, the problem is probably underneath it — and rebuilding the base may be more important than patching what you can see.

### Start with what changed

Look at the overall shape first.

Are the steps still level?

Has a walkway developed a dip?

Is a wall leaning?

Has a gap opened between the masonry and the house?

Then look closer at the material itself.

You may see:

Mortar falling out of the joints.

Brick faces flaking or cracking.

Loose bricks or stones.

Rocking or sunken pavers.

Cracks running through several courses.

Water collecting around the problem area.

Those details help separate a surface repair from movement underneath.

### When repointing may be enough

Mortar is supposed to take wear before the brick does. If the bricks are sound and the wall or steps haven't moved, deteriorated mortar can often be removed and replaced. Individual damaged bricks can often be replaced at the same time. The result preserves most of the existing masonry instead of rebuilding it unnecessarily.

### When the base is the problem

A surface patch won't last if the structure underneath is moving. Steps that have pulled away from the house, widespread sunken pavers, or a leaning wall may need to come apart so the base can be corrected. Good brick, stone, or pavers can sometimes be reused during that work.

### Water matters

A lot of masonry problems have water somewhere in the story. Downspouts can discharge beside steps. A walkway can pitch toward the house. Water can enter deteriorated joints and freeze during winter. Fixing the masonry without addressing an obvious water problem can lead to the same failure again. When you send the project, include the nearest downspout and tell us where water goes after a heavy rain.

### Winter repairs

Exterior mortar work needs suitable temperatures while the mortar cures. That can limit when some repairs are performed in Northern New Jersey. An unsafe step or loose masonry can still be assessed sooner, even when the permanent repair needs better weather.

### When to look closer

A noticeably leaning retaining wall, major movement, or cracking that appears connected to the home's foundation can be more than a routine masonry repair. In those cases, the right first step may be determining whether a structural assessment is needed.

### Is regrouting enough when grout starts crumbling?

Regrouting may be appropriate when the tiles and the surface beneath them are sound. Loose tiles, recurring cracks, soft surfaces or moisture can change that assessment. Share the wider area as well as the joints so the provider can consider the cause before recommending a cosmetic repair.

Grout that's powdery, cracked, or missing between otherwise solid tiles.

Tiles move when pressed.

Cracks run in a line across the floor.

Grout takes a lot of wear, especially on floors and in showers. Over time it can break down from traffic, water exposure, cleaning products, or simply age.
