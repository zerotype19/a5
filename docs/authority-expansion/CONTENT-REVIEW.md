Identify any access opening needed for another trade and coordinate the order of those visits. If you want a removable access panel, discuss its location and appearance before the surface is closed.

### Define where the repair ends

A proposal should distinguish removing damaged material, replacing board, joint finishing, texture work, priming and painting. Ask whether the price includes finishing the whole ceiling or wall, or only the patched area.

Share any leftover paint details and your expectations for how the repair should look in daylight. Discuss furniture protection, dust management and cleanup. Do not assume that a drywall repair automatically includes a full room repaint.

### Keep the handoff simple

Keep the cause assessment and repair scope together. Identify who confirms the area is ready for the finishing trade, and ask how newly discovered damage changes the proposal. A5 can help route the request, but the relevant provider must assess the actual conditions.

When submitting the request, include the room, the visible damage, the leak history, any completed corrective work and whether you need painting included. Those details reduce repeated questions between trades.

Sources: [EPA mold cleanup guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/what-are-basic-mold-cleanup-steps)

## How to prepare for a tile repair

/guides/prepare-for-a-tile-repair · insert

**Description:** Plan a tile repair around spare materials, the extent of damage, grout matching, access and the condition behind the visible finish.

**What information helps a provider assess a tile repair?**

Show the whole tiled area and the damaged section, say whether tiles are loose or cracked, and mention any recurring moisture. Check for spare tiles and packaging, but let the provider assess compatibility. Ask whether the scope covers only the visible finish or also the underlying surface.

### Separate appearance from a recurring problem

A missing grout joint, chipped tile and loose section of flooring need different conversations. Describe what changed and whether the same spot has been repaired before. Include water-related observations without assuming grout alone will address them.

For a shower or other wet area, ask the provider to assess whether the issue extends beyond the visible surface. A photograph cannot establish the condition of concealed materials or waterproofing.

### Find any spare material

Look for labeled boxes, leftover tile, grout records or purchase receipts. Photograph the front and any identifying packaging. A nominal size or similar color does not establish an exact match, so discuss samples before ordering a quantity.

If a close match cannot be found, ask about the visual alternatives before approving removal. Depending on the location, the discussion may include a larger replacement area or a deliberate change in finish rather than a small mismatched patch.

### Clarify removal, repair and finishing

Ask how much of the area will be opened, what underlying repair is included, and how unexpected conditions will be handled. Identify transitions, trim, fixtures and adjoining surfaces that might be affected.

Ask who moves fixtures, supplies materials and removes debris. If another trade is involved, agree on the sequence. A tile repair proposal should make it clear where the tile work ends and any plumbing or carpentry work begins.

### Plan for access and return to use

Tell the provider whether this is the home's only bathroom or a frequently used entrance. Ask when the surface can be used again for the actual products and method specified; avoid planning around a generic online drying time.

Share photos, dimensions if you have them, the spare material list and your finish priorities with A5. The provider can then confirm whether a targeted repair is practical and what needs to be assessed in person.

## Prepare for a scheduled plumbing visit

/guides/prepare-for-a-plumbing-service-visit · insert

**Description:** Describe fixture problems clearly, gather useful product details and compare repair or replacement scopes before a scheduled plumbing visit.

**What should I send before a scheduled plumbing visit?**

Identify the fixture or visible pipe, explain when the issue occurs and share photos without dismantling anything. Mention active water leakage, access limitations, previous repairs and any replacement product you have chosen. A5 reviews scheduled requests; it is not an emergency dispatch service.

### Describe the behavior, not just the fixture

For a toilet, say whether it runs continuously, runs intermittently, leaks visibly or has another symptom. For a faucet, distinguish a drip from the outlet from water around the base or cabinet. Describe what you can observe safely rather than guessing which part failed.

State when the problem started and whether more than one fixture is affected. If water is actively causing damage or creating an immediate hazard, seek appropriate urgent assistance instead of waiting for a scheduled A5 request.

### Gather useful product and access details

Photograph the fixture and the surrounding space. Add model information or packaging you already have; there is no need to remove covers or disconnect anything to identify a part. Say whether the fixture is difficult to reach or whether furniture or stored items affect access.

If you are considering replacement, share the product before ordering. Ask the professional to confirm fit, connections and any additional work involved. A product described as a replacement may still have a different installation scope.

### Compare repair and replacement scopes

Ask the provider to explain the proposed work and which parts are included. A repair proposal and a replacement proposal are not directly comparable if one includes additional supply, drain or finish work.

Confirm who supplies the fixture, who handles removal and disposal, and whether associated wall, cabinet or tile repair is included. Ask who will determine applicable permit and inspection requirements for the actual scope.

### Agree on the next step

Keep the scope, assumptions and exclusions in writing. If access reveals another condition, ask how the provider will explain and obtain approval for additional work. Confirm scheduling with the provider after the introduction.

Your A5 request can start with the fixture, location, symptoms and optional photos. You do not need a technical diagnosis or a complete shopping list before getting help.

## Plan a lighting or electrical service request

/guides/plan-lighting-and-electrical-updates · insert

**Description:** Organize lighting, outlet and fan requests with clear symptoms, product details, access needs and questions for the electrical professional.

**What should I include in an electrical service request?**

Describe the affected light, outlet, switch or fan and when the issue occurs. Share product details for planned replacements and note whether other devices are affected. Do not remove covers or test wiring for photos. The electrical professional determines the assessment and installation requirements.

### Distinguish a repair from an upgrade

An existing light that stopped working and a new fixture in a new position are different requests. State which one you need and whether the existing controls are changing. If several devices are affected, include that observation rather than treating each as an unrelated repair.

If there is smoke, sparking, a burning smell or another immediate hazard, keep away and seek appropriate urgent assistance. A5 coordinates scheduled work and does not provide emergency dispatch.

### Share the product before the visit

For a new light or fan, include its model and installation information if available. Explain the intended location and whether the item has already been purchased. Let the professional assess support, controls, compatibility and the existing installation.

Photograph the area from a safe position. Do not remove an outlet cover, open equipment or expose wiring for the request. A wide room photo can help communicate access and location without creating an unnecessary task.

### Clarify what the installation includes

Ask whether the proposal includes removal, assembly, installation, testing and disposal. If the work involves a new location or access through a finished surface, ask what wall or ceiling repair is excluded and who will complete it.

Discuss how the provider will assess permit requirements and any inspections. Avoid assuming that a product change is automatically the same scope as the previous installation.

### Coordinate the room as well as the electrical work

Tell the provider about high ceilings, furniture, restricted access and other trades involved. Agree on product delivery and what must be ready before the visit. Have the intended fixture locations and control preferences documented.

A5 can review your request and coordinate an appropriate local professional. The professional confirms the work, required credentials, price and timing; a service page does not establish a booked appointment or specific availability.

Sources: [ESFI Electrical Safety Dos](https://www.esfi.org/electrical-safety-dos/)
