# Content foundation and writing templates

Use the same voice as the homepage: direct, warm, specific and useful. Start with the homeowner’s problem. Explain what happens next. A5 coordinates introductions; providers discuss and perform the work.

## Service page brief

1. Name the work and the homeowner’s desired outcome in one H1.
2. In 40–70 words, explain common requests and A5’s next step.
3. List three to five concrete jobs the service covers. Avoid repeating the title as filler.
4. Describe the useful photos/details to send and what the provider will assess.
5. Explain scope, estimate and scheduling without inventing a response deadline or price.
6. Answer the few questions that affect a homeowner’s decision. Show one direct answer, not two versions of the same answer in adjacent panels.
7. Link relevant problem pages, guides and towns from the published content registry.
8. End with the service-specific request action.

## Problem / guide brief

- H1: a question or observable problem, not a generic keyword list.
- Direct answer: two to four sentences that answer the primary question without pretending to diagnose a property remotely.
- Sections: what you may notice; what to discuss with a professional; options and limits; photos that help; next step.
- Write connected paragraphs. Use a list for parallel checks, not a separate paragraph for every short sentence.
- Cite factual/regulatory/technical claims in the existing source system. Show genuine dates only. Avoid unsourced cost ranges and universal permit statements.
- Keep draft material in review until its evidence and business claims are approved. Layout work never changes publication status.

## Example editorial revision: masonry hub (draft, not published)

**Opening:** Loose mortar, a rocking paver or brick steps that have started to crumble can make the front of your home harder to use. Tell A5 what has changed and share a few photos. We will review the request and check for an appropriate local masonry provider.

**What to show us:** Start with a wide photo of the steps, walkway or wall, then add a close-up of the affected area. Include a side view if the surface looks tilted or uneven. Mention whether the change appeared suddenly, has grown over time or is more noticeable after rain.

**The conversation with the provider:** A provider can assess the condition in person and discuss the scope, repair options, estimate and timing. A photo helps start that conversation; it does not confirm the cause or the final repair. Availability is confirmed after review.
